title: Persistent Volumes
description: Keep files and data alive across redeploys for stateful apps.

By default, a container's filesystem is wiped on every redeploy — you get a
fresh start each time. That's perfect for stateless apps, but a problem if your
app stores files locally (uploads, a SQLite database, generated assets).

**Volumes** solve this: they're storage that lives *outside* the container and
survives redeploys.

## When you need a volume

Use a volume when your app writes data to disk that must persist, such as:

- User-uploaded files
- A local SQLite database
- Generated caches or assets you don't want to rebuild
- Any state that should outlive a single deploy

<Callout type="info" title="Prefer a managed database when you can">
  For relational data, an attached [managed database](/docs/guides/databases) is
  usually a better choice than a volume + SQLite — it's easier to back up and
  scale. Use volumes for files and genuinely file-based state.
</Callout>

## Declaring a volume

<Steps>
<Step>
Open your app and go to the **Configuration** tab.
</Step>
<Step>
Add a volume in the form `name:/container/path`, for example:

```text
uploads:/app/data
```

This maps a persistent volume named `uploads` to the `/app/data` directory
inside your container.
</Step>
<Step>
Save and redeploy. Anything your app writes to `/app/data` now survives future
redeploys.
</Step>
</Steps>

## How it works

The part before the colon is the **volume name** (a stable identifier
Better-PaaS keeps around). The part after the colon is the **path inside your
container** where it's mounted. On redeploy, the same named volume is re-attached
to the new container at that path, so your data is right where you left it.
